About me

A little about me

My passion for science started early on. Even at school, I could never quite get enough of maths and physics, which led me to participate in academic Olympiads and eventually pursue my studies in the UK. I've always enjoyed the challenge of understanding how things work and finding solutions to difficult problems, a curiosity that ultimately led me to a PhD in theoretical condensed matter physics at the University of Manchester.

Alongside my academic research, I also became involved in consulting projects, which introduced me to a different side of problem-solving. I began to appreciate not just the science behind an idea, but also its practical applications and the challenges of bringing it into the real world. These experiences gradually shaped my decision to move into industry, where I could combine my scientific background with a more applied approach.

In a way, my journey has brought me back to where I started. Quantum computing was one of my earliest research interests, dating back to my first undergraduate research mentorship. Today, I'm returning to that field with a much broader background in quantum physics, developing quantum algorithms for computer-aided engineering and computational fluid dynamics.

Outside of work, I love spending time outdoors and getting lost in the mountains, whether that's hiking, heading off on multi-day treks, tackling a via ferrata route or skiing in winter. When I can't escape to the mountains, I enjoy playing squash and indoor bouldering. I also love going to live musicals and concerts, getting creative with design, 3D printing and LEGO, getting lost in a good dystopian novel, or challenging myself with a puzzle or strategy board game.

Publications

My published research in theoretical condensed matter physics.

PAPER / 01

Anomalous thermoelectric Hall response of interacting 2D Dirac fermions

A. Daria Dumitriu-I., Feng Liu, Alexander E. Kazantsev, Alessandro Principi · arXiv · 2026

READ MORE HERE ↗

We study how interactions between electrons influence the sideways flow of heat and charge in a two-dimensional quantum system, even without an external magnetic field. After carefully removing contributions that do not represent actual transport, we find that the interacting thermoelectric Hall response unexpectedly remains non-zero as the temperature approaches absolute zero. We show that this surprising behaviour arises from quantum effects at extremely small length scales that influence the system's large-scale properties.

PAPER / 02

Broken Lorentz symmetry and violation of the Wiedemann-Franz law in topological insulators

Feng Liu, A. Daria Dumitriu-I., Alessandro Principi · Physical Review B · 2025

READ MORE HERE ↗

We study how temperature variations affect heat transport in a two-dimensional topological insulator, using a static nonuniform gravitational field to model these variations. We show that breaking the Lorentz symmetry by adding a Newtonian mass term to the theoretical model fundamentally changes its quantum behaviour, leading to a heat current flowing through the material's interior that violates the usual relationship between thermal and electrical conductivity.

PAPER / 03

No bulk thermal currents in massive Dirac fermions

Feng Liu, A. Daria Dumitriu-I., Alessandro Principi · Physical Review B · 2024

READ MORE HERE ↗

We study how energy flows through the interior and along the boundaries of a two-dimensional quantum system with an interface where the electron mass changes sign. We resolve a longstanding debate in the literature by showing that, at low temperatures and over large length scales, temperature differences do not produce a thermal Hall current in the interior of the material. Instead, this current arises entirely from quantum states localised at the interface, correcting earlier theoretical predictions.

PAPER / 04

First-order effect of electron-electron interactions on the anomalous Hall conductivity of massive Dirac fermions

A. Daria Dumitriu-I., Darius-A. Deaconu, Alexander E. Kazantsev, and Alessandro Principi · Physical Review B · 2024

READ MORE HERE ↗

We study how electron interactions affect transverse electrical transport phenomena in two-dimensional quantum system without an applied magnetic field. We show that these interactions do not alter the topological quantum behaviour expected at zero temperature in a fully gapped system, confirming a fundamental theoretical prediction. However, at finite temperatures or material doping, interactions cause the Hall response to decrease more rapidly than in the noninteracting case, with potentially observable experimental consequences.

PhD thesis

A closer look at my PhD research in theoretical condensed matter physics at the University of Manchester and the Graphene NOWNANO Centre for Doctoral Training.

DEFENDED · MAY 2026

The interplay between topology, geometry and correlations in anomalous thermoelectric transport

Alessandro Principi (Main Supervisor) · Mohammad Saeed Bahramy (Co-Supervisor) · Vladimir Falko (Co-Supervisor)
READ THE FULL THESIS HERE ↗

Abstract

This thesis studies anomalous thermoelectric transport in two-dimensional systems with broken time-reversal symmetry, focusing on the interplay between topology, band geometry, and electron-electron interactions. We develop a general theoretical framework to distinguish genuine transport from currents that circulate within the material, and then investigate how electron interactions affect these transport phenomena. We show that the topological electrical Hall response remains unchanged at very low temperatures in fully gapped systems, but can be significantly reduced outside this regime. Surprisingly, we find that existing theoretical methods fail to correctly describe certain thermoelectric effects when electron interactions are included, highlighting the need to revise the current theoretical framework.

Consulting & research projects

Beyond my doctoral research, I've had the opportunity to explore a range of projects, from consulting and research commercialisation to interdisciplinary CDT projects and earlier undergraduate work.

Consulting & Commercialisation

Alongside my academic work, I had the chance to take part in two substantial consulting projects, one with Cambridge EnterpriseTECH and another with the Manchester branch of 180 Degrees Consulting. As part of the Cambridge EnterpriseTECH programme, I also completed a ten-week course in research commercialisation at Cambridge Judge Business School, University of Cambridge. Together, these experiences gave me the opportunity to approach problems from a different perspective, explore how scientific ideas can be developed into real-world applications, and learn more about the commercial side of research.

01 / CONSULTING PROJECT

Cambridge EnterpriseTECH

Cohort 14 · Cambridge Judge Business School · Cambridge, UK
EnterpriseTECH Programme ↗

As part of the EnterpriseTECH programme at Cambridge Judge Business School (Cohort 14), I worked in a multidisciplinary team on a project for Cambridge Surgical Models, an early-stage startup developing realistic artificial anatomical models for surgical training and medical education. Over the 10-week programme, we explored the commercial potential of their technology, surveying and interviewing a combined total of almost 200 medical students, doctors and medical educators, and researching the competitive landscape, potential customers and routes to market. We used this research to assess the product's commercial feasibility, estimate the UK market size and develop recommendations for the startup's business strategy. Our final deliverables included a detailed commercial feasibility report, a short promotional infomercial and a pitch deck, which we presented to a panel of judges.

02 / CONSULTING PROJECT

180 Degrees Consulting

University of Manchester Branch · Manchester, UK
180DC Manchester Branch ↗

As part of 180 Degrees Consulting Manchester, I worked on a project for HippocratesAI, a startup developing AI-powered tools for healthcare and medical education. Our team surveyed around 50 medical students and healthcare professionals to better understand their experiences with AI, their needs and concerns, and what they would look for in such a product. We also researched competing technologies, compared different UK healthcare markets, and explored potential routes into the NHS, GP practices and universities. We brought all this together into a detailed report and presentation with recommendations on the company's market entry and growth strategy.
